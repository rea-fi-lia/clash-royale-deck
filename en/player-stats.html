How to check your own win rate and battle record in Clash Royale

Use your player tag to check your recent win rate, results for each deck you used, and difficult matchups. This guide explains how to use CRDB and interpret the numbers when improving your deck.

1. Open your battle record using your player tag

  1. Find the player tag beginning with # in your in-game Clash Royale profile. It is a separate ID from your display name.
  2. Open My page in CRDB, enter your tag and press Register and view. You can leave out the #.
  3. Check the player name, then choose an available period: 1 day, 1 week, 1 month or 1 year.
  4. Check wins, losses and battle counts under Your record, and results for each deck under Performance by deck used.

You only need a tag to view the results. Logging in lets you use the saved tag across devices. Entering a tag does not prove ownership of the game account. If the wrong player appears, select Change tag and enter it again.

2. Read win rate together with the period, battle count and game modes

CRDB personal stats cover collected 1v1 battles played during the selected period. Win rate is wins ÷ collected battles × 100, with draws included in the denominator. The win/loss totals combine multiple game modes; they are not a Ranked-only win rate.

Example calculation (fictional):With 14 wins, 10 losses and 1 draw, the calculation is 14 ÷ 25 × 100 = 56%. Excluding draws or using a different period changes the result, even for the same player.

The trophy chart gives priority to Ranked records. Even when the chart shows Ranked values, the win/loss totals are not necessarily limited to Ranked. Check the battle count and period shown on the page first.

Win rates based on a few battles can change sharply. For example, 3 wins in 4 battles is 75%, but one more loss brings it down to 60%. Do not judge your skill or a deck from a short period alone. Consider the battle details alongside results from periods when you played the same mode.

3. Use results by deck to consider card changes

The deck section matches wins and losses to the 8 cards you actually used. Simply picking the deck with the highest win rate can overvalue decks with few recorded battles or a run of favorable matchups.

  • Compare the same period and check how many battles you played with each deck.
  • Check differences in card levels, Evolution and Hero forms, and the game modes played.
  • Keep a copy of the original 8 cards, then change one card at a time to address the role your deck is missing.
  • Alongside wins and losses, review the situations where you lost, such as failing to defend against air attacks in time or being stopped by buildings.

For example, a higher win rate after replacing an anti-air card does not prove that the replacement caused the improvement. Opposing decks and card levels also change. Use deck analysis to check how the roles have changed, then look for improvements in actual battles to help decide your next adjustment.

4. Distinguish the matchups you lost from those popular in the wider meta

Your battle meta shows the opponents you actually faced. Results by opposing card count wins and losses against decks containing that card. They do not mean that the card alone caused the loss.

Your trophy range right now or Ranked right now shows a separate set of data collected by CRDB. For Trophy Road, it shows the relevant trophy range; for Ranked, it shows all collected Ranked battles. The period, player count and modes may differ from your personal stats.

First, look at difficult matchups you have played often, then review missing roles such as air defense, buildings or small spells. Use popularity and win rates in the wider meta to find candidates to try next.

5. When battles are missing or the numbers are not updating

We use recent battles available on your first visit along with battles already saved in CRDB. The game API does not provide your entire past history, so choosing 1 year does not mean a complete year of records is available.

For general users, registering a tag does not start automatic hourly collection. Battles are mainly fetched when you open the page, so records from periods when you did not visit may be missing.

Check the retrieval status and the last retrieval time on the page. If fetching new battles fails, saved records are shown. If requests are limited or a temporary problem occurs, wait a while and try again.

If there are few records, try a longer period and check the tag and player name. Treat the figures as results from the records available. Do not compare them directly with lifetime wins in the game or differently calculated figures on other sites without matching the conditions.

FAQ

Where can I check my own Clash Royale win rate?

Enter your player tag on My page in CRDB to see your win rate by period from collected battles. Choose an available period, such as 1 week, and read the win rate together with the battle count.

Can I see the win rate for each deck?

Performance by deck used shows results from collected battles grouped by deck. Changing the period changes which battles are included.

Does having no battles mean a 0% win rate?

Having no collected battles is different from playing battles without winning. First, check whether any records are available and look at the retrieval status.

Can I see every battle from before I registered?

We look for matching battles already saved in CRDB, but cannot guarantee a complete history from before registration. We do not estimate missing battles or add them to your win rate.
